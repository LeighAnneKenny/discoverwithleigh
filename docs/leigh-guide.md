# Your off-site growth guide

This guide is for you, Leigh — no tech skills needed. It covers three things you can do outside the website to bring in more clients, especially tourists visiting Cape Town. Work through it in an afternoon.

1. Google Business Profile — get found on Google Maps.
2. WhatsApp auto-replies — greet enquirers even when you're busy.
3. A consistency checklist — keep your details the same everywhere.

---

## 1. Google Business Profile

### Why this matters

When a tourist searches "photographer near me" or "Cape Town photographer" on their phone, Google usually shows the Maps results first — above your website. Your Google Business Profile is often the very first thing a new client sees. It also feeds Google's AI answers, so it helps you show up when people ask an AI assistant for photographer recommendations.

### How to set it up

1. Go to **business.google.com** and sign in with your business Google account.
2. Search for "Discover With Leigh." If it already exists, claim it. If not, create it.
3. **Choose your categories.** Pick "Photographer" as your main category. Add "Wedding photographer" as a second category. You can add more later (e.g. "Portrait photographer").
4. **Set your service area.** Choose Cape Town and the surrounding areas you're willing to travel to. Don't set a single fixed address unless you have a public studio — a service area works better for a mobile photographer.
5. **Add your website link** — the business's website URL.
6. **Fill in your business hours** and a short description of what you do (weddings, portraits, vacation shoots, brand/lifestyle work).
7. **Add photos regularly.** Upload a fresh batch every few weeks — real shoots, not stock images. Profiles with recent photos rank higher and convert better.
8. **Keep your name, phone number, and area identical** to what's on your website and social pages. See the checklist in section 3.

### Build the review habit

Reviews on Google (not on your own website) are what Google — and AI assistants — trust most, because they're written by someone else, not by you. After every happy client:

- Ask them, in person or in your thank-you message, to leave a Google review.
- Make it easy: Google gives you a shareable review link from your Business Profile dashboard — send it directly.
- Do this every time. A steady trickle of reviews works better than a single burst.

## 2. WhatsApp Business auto-replies

Your WhatsApp Business app can send automatic replies. There are two kinds: a **greeting message** for new enquirers, and an **away message** for when you're out. Both are set up in the same place.

**Where to set them:** open WhatsApp Business → **Settings → Business tools**.

Helpful links from WhatsApp's own help pages:

- Greeting message: https://faq.whatsapp.com/501866148528310
- Away message: https://faq.whatsapp.com/2565868990219715

### Greeting message

This sends automatically to someone messaging you for the first time (or after 14 days of silence). It's what people see right after tapping the website's WhatsApp button.

- Set recipients to **"Everyone."**
- Starting draft (edit freely, in your own voice):

  > "Hi! Thanks for reaching out to Discover With Leigh 📸 I've seen your message and I'll get back to you personally within a few hours — usually much sooner. To speed things along, tell me a bit about what you have in mind: the type of shoot or campaign, and any dates you're eyeing."

### Away message

This covers you when you're out of reach — for example, mid-shoot — for people messaging you again after the greeting. Turning on a set schedule (e.g. "outside business hours") requires business hours to be set on your profile first (see section 1, step 6).

- Starting draft:

  > "Thanks for your message! I'm out on a shoot right now 🎥 — I'll reply as soon as I'm back. If it's urgent, you can also email [the business email]."

### Decisions only you can make

- **Response-time promise.** Only promise a turnaround you'll actually keep. If "a few hours" isn't realistic, change it — a promise you can't keep is worse than no promise.
- **Away schedule vs. always-on.** Do you want the away message to fire only outside set business hours, or all the time whenever you haven't replied yet? Your call.
- **Emoji tone.** The drafts above use a couple of emoji. Keep, trim, or drop them to match how you actually talk to clients.
- **Business profile completeness.** Make sure hours, a short description, and the website link are filled in — the away schedule depends on the hours being set, and a complete profile looks more trustworthy.

We looked at WhatsApp's paid Business Platform/API (for bigger automated setups) and decided against it — it's built for teams, not a one-person studio.

## 3. Consistency checklist

Google, and any AI assistant summarising your business, cross-checks your details across the web. Mismatches make you look less trustworthy and can hurt how easily you're found. Make sure these are word-for-word identical everywhere:

- [ ] **Business name** — same spelling, same capitalisation, on the website, Google Business Profile, WhatsApp, Instagram, and TikTok.
- [ ] **Phone number** — same format (e.g. always with the country code) on all five.
- [ ] **Service area** — "Cape Town" (plus whatever surrounding areas you list) worded the same way everywhere.

Check this list every time you update a profile anywhere — one change often needs to happen in all five places.
